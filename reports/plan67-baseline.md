## 0.3 — the cause of G-UX-01, on the demo

**Method:**
- `curl.exe -sI` and `curl.exe -D -` on `/careers`, `/careers/track`, `/` and `/ui/hr-core/`.
- `AC_UX_67_vps_every_key_page_carries_one_policy_and_raises_no_violation`, with `UX067_VPS_LABEL=before`. It signs in as `hradmin` in Chromium (the portal stays anonymous). For each key page of P-12 it records the CSP headers of the main response and every `securitypolicyviolation` event, through an init script registered before the page's markup is parsed. It takes screenshots at 1366×768 and 390×844.
- Output: `artifacts/plan67/vps/before/` (`csp-pages.json`, 44 screenshots). Run time: 5 min 28 s.

**Result: the two-policies hypothesis is proven.**
- Every page the gateway forwards arrives with **two** `Content-Security-Policy` headers:
  - the gateway's, written by `UseHrSecurityHeaders` before `MapReverseProxy` (its `connect-src` names the SSO host);
  - the service's, which YARP adds next to it.
- The other security headers are doubled the same way: `X-Frame-Options`, `X-Content-Type-Options`, `Referrer-Policy`, `Permissions-Policy`.
- An inline block must satisfy both policies, and it carries only the service's nonce, so the gateway's policy blocks it.
- The gateway's own home page carries one policy and raises no violation.

**What is blocked on every service page:**
- line 13: the agency colour (`_OrganizationBranding`'s `<style>`);
- line 18: its script;
- the page's own inline blocks.

**Two causes, both closed in P1:**
1. **The second policy.** It blocks every inline block: the portal's styles, the agency colour, the charts, the hire mode switch.
2. **The inline handlers and `style` attributes.** A nonce never covers these, so they are blocked even under one policy.

**Side finding:** `HEAD /careers` answers 302 to sign-in, while `GET /careers` answers 200. The public route matches GET only. This does not affect a browser.
